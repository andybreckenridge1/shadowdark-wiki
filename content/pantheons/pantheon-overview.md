The Great Cataclysm and the Great War transformed religion throughout the former Assyrian Empire. Many Balkan peoples have lost faith in the Olympian gods, believing they failed mortals during the disasters and the ensuing war. Athena remains a notable exception. The cults of the Three Heroes (or demigods)—Hercules, Alleah, and the Gray Mouse—now dominate much of the region.

Nevertheless, mortals continue to seek the favor of the older gods through prayer, sacrifice, and offerings. Sailors seek Poseidon’s blessing before voyages, farmers make sacrifices to Demeter at planting time, and smiths invoke Hephaestus at their forges. However, temples dedicated to these gods have become increasingly uncommon.

Gaia, the ancient mother of the Titans and ancestor of the Olympians, is historically associated with Lucius’s pact and the Great Cataclysm. The Titans and the sorceress Circe are believed to have prevented the Olympians from intervening during the Great War.

Elsewhere, older religious traditions persist, and some are experiencing a revival. Many priests possess signature spells reflecting the nature and powers of their patron deity.
